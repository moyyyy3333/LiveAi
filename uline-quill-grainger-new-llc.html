What new LLCs actually get from starter net-30 vendors

Ignition · updated 14 September 2026 · 7 min read

A short list of vendor names circulates in almost every "business credit" guide: Uline, Quill, Grainger. They're mentioned because they're real — new-entity-friendly suppliers that some LLCs can get net-30 terms from early. What most guides skip is what actually happens on the way there. Here's the honest version.

Uline

Uline is generally one of the more reachable starter vendors for a brand-new LLC. In practice, a first order from a new entity is often shipped prepaid, with invoice/net-30 terms requested and considered on a later order once there's some purchase history. A prepaid first invoice is not an open net-30 line — it's a normal first transaction. Don't count it as "approved" until the terms request on a subsequent order actually comes back as terms.

Quill

Quill often surfaces net-30 as an option at checkout for qualifying business accounts, sometimes with a merchandise minimum (commonly cited around $100). Whether that account activity gets reported to Dun & Bradstreet is frequently tied to a separate opt-in trade-reporting program, and may only apply above a certain invoice threshold. Even when it's reported, bureau posting can lag 60–90 days behind the actual transaction. The guarantee that matters is "the vendor granted terms," not "the bureau published a line" — those are different clocks, and only the first one is something you can act on right away.

Grainger

Grainger reads more like an industrial-supply account than a purpose-built starter tradeline. The typical path is creating a customer account first, then requesting net-30 terms, with underwriting that may reference a D&B file. A two-week-old LLC with no purchase history and no D&B record yet is a common candidate for a "not yet, reapply after some activity" response rather than an outright denial. That's a real, if unglamorous, outcome — and it's worth planning for rather than being surprised by.

The rule that actually protects you here

Only count written invoice or net-30 terms in the LLC's name as a win. A "pay this order, apply again later" response is not a line — it's useful information, and often the expected first answer, not a failure.

What actually moves the outcome

  • The legal name, address, and phone on the application match your EIN and bank account exactly — no retyping from memory.
  • The address can receive a package. A vendor that can't verify deliverability will decline or delay, regardless of everything else being in order.
  • You place and pay a small first order from the LLC's own checking account, not a personal card.
  • You apply again on the vendor's terms, on their timeline — not once, out of order, hoping for the best.
