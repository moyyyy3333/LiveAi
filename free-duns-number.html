How to request a D-U-N-S Number without buying a reseller

Ignition · updated 14 September 2026 · 5 min read

A D-U-N-S Number is how Dun & Bradstreet files a company in its database — the record that vendors and lenders check when they underwrite a business. It is often the first "credit identity" a new LLC gets, and it's the one most commonly upsold.

The D-U-N-S request at dnb.com is free. Issuance is commonly measured in weeks, not hours. That wait is annoying — it is not a reason to pay a reseller.

How to request it

  1. Go to dnb.com and search for "D-U-N-S Number request" (D&B's iUpdate / D-U-N-S request tool).
  2. Enter your LLC's legal name, address, and phone number exactly as they appear on your EIN confirmation and formation document — the same NAP pattern you locked earlier.
  3. Submit the free request. You'll get a confirmation reference — save it.
  4. Expect the number itself to take on the order of weeks to issue. D&B does not publish a guaranteed turnaround, and neither can anyone selling you one.

Why the "faster DUNS" resellers are a trap, not a shortcut

Paid DUNS-acceleration products sell two things: speed, and a formatted application. Neither changes D&B's own verification process. What they can do is create a second submission of your company's information, filed slightly differently than your own free request — a second version of the identity you are trying to keep consistent everywhere else.

If you're running Ignition's Sprint Pass, this matters for a concrete reason: paid "faster DUNS" products void the 30-day guarantee. Not because we're strict for its own sake — because a forked identity is the single most common reason a vendor's underwriting can't match your file to your LLC.

What to do while you wait

The D-U-N-S request doesn't have to finish before you move forward. Submit it early (day 3–4 of a 30-day sprint is typical), then keep working: finish business checking, submit your vendor applications, and place your first small orders. Some vendors extend terms without waiting on a published D&B score at all — trade credit is a supplier's decision, not a bureau's.

Common mistakes

Submitting the DUNS request with a slightly different name or address than the one on your EIN and bank account.
Paying for "priority processing" from a site that isn't dnb.com.
Waiting on the DUNS number before submitting vendor applications — most don't require it to apply.
Requesting a second DUNS number later because the first "seems stuck" — this creates duplicate records that take far longer to untangle than the wait itself.
